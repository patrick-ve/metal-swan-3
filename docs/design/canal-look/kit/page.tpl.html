Four Canal Scenes

I measured the backgrounds of the arcade reference in the screenshots you gave. Then I painted four places in Amsterdam by the rules that came out: two canals, the river, and the pond of the museum square. All scenery here is new. It uses no art of the game and no art of the reference.

New in this version: the second scene is now Museumplein in autumn, with the same sky, light and air as before. The fourth scene is now De Wallen, the Red Light district, by night. The scenery has three resolutions. Vincent is unchanged.

Scenery resolution
The switch changes all four screens. Each tier is painted for its grid: nothing is scaled up.

What the higher tiers add

1 pxreal pixel steps

Curves and slopes

Gables, arches, hulls, wheels, roofs and tree crowns step by one real pixel. At 1× they step by one large pixel.

½cell size

Texture and dither

Bricks, roof tiles, leaf clusters, grit and the dither checker use cells of half the size. At 4× the brick joints show. The noise of clouds, leaves, bark and water gets one or two finer layers.

½stroke width

Thin parts

Glazing bars, rails, spokes, rigging, twigs and stone joints are half as wide. So a window has finer bars, not thicker ones.

=same palette

What does not change

The ramps, the key light, the air per plane and the layout are the same at each tier. The number of colours in a frame stays almost the same.

What the reference does

Each number below comes from the screenshots. Each line ends with the rule that I applied in the canal scenes.

122colours in a frame

A small palette, used hard

The one lossless screenshot has 122 colours in the full frame at its own grid (304 px wide). Each colour sits on a grid of 5 bits per channel. Rule: every colour in the scenes is snapped to that grid, and every surface takes its tones from one short ramp.

10tones in the sky

The sky is a ramp of the smallest steps

Ten tones cover 90 % of that sky. Neighbour tones differ by one step of the colour grid. Rule: skies of 12 to 18 tones, never a smooth gradient.

34 %of sky pixels

Dither only where air is

A third of the sky pixels lie in a 1-pixel checker between two tones. The band borders are torn and follow the cloud streaks. Objects have no dither: they have clean clusters. Rule: checker dither on sky, cloud bases, light halos and smoke only.

59–96 %one hue family

One key hue for each stage

I took the pixels with colour and counted those within 25° of the strongest hue: beach 59 %, dunes 71 %, dusk sky 87 %, orange stage 95 %, factory 96 %, night 96 %. Rule: each scene gets one key light that pulls sky, water, stone and brick together.

181°far hue = sky hue

Far things take the colour of the air

On the beach, the far wreck has the same hue as the sky at the horizon (181°). Its saturation is 0.34. The near wreck has hue 33° and saturation 0.59. Rule: each plane is mixed toward the air colour of its hour, more with distance.

0.11 → 0.62light range, far → near

Contrast grows toward the viewer

In the dunes, the far ridge spans 0.11 of the light range, the walk dune 0.23, the front dune 0.62. The darkest tone of the far wreck is 0.33, of the near wreck 0.09. Rule: the deepest darks are in the front plane only.

4–6tones in a cloud

Clouds are solid bodies

A lit top, a shaded flat base, and a base that goes into the sky through dither. The shade side takes the sky hue. Rule: clouds are built from spheres on a flat base, lit from one side, cut to 5 or 6 tones.

1 pxrim and edge

Edges are tones, not black lines

The dark edge of a thing is the dark tone of its own colour, on the shade side. The sun side gets a thin light rim. Every surface has wear: stains, moss, cracks, rivets. Rule: the same on bricks, stone, hulls and leaves.

3 + 1planes and a frame

Depth is built from planes

A sky, a far plane, the walk plane, and dark things at the lower edge that pass faster than the hero. The ground is a plane: its detail gets larger toward the viewer. Rule: seven to nine layers per scene, the water scrolls row by row.

96 % / 0scene / hero

Figures keep their own colours

In the night stage the scenery is one blue. The figures stay warm, so the eye finds them at once. Rule: Vincent is never graded with the scene.

The ten sky tones of the lossless screenshot, dark to light. Each step is 8 of 255 in one or more channels.

How the scenes are made

  • A paint kit draws each scene in the 384 × 216 layout. The tier says how many real pixels one layout pixel has.
  • Parts: canal houses with five gable types, shutters and stoops; a museum front; elms; boats; quay walls; bridges; street furniture; neon signs; swans and ducks.
  • Each scene paints in daylight colours first. Then one colour table per plane gives the hour and the air.
  • The water mirrors the far bank, squeezes it, and breaks it with ripples. It has three frames.
  • At night, light falls on what is there. The wall around a red room steps toward red in two tones, and the detail of the wall stays.

What moves

  • Parallax: sky 0, clouds 0.02–0.03, skyline 0.10–0.18, far bank 0.3–0.5, walk plane 1, front things 1.35.
  • Water: each row has its own speed, from the far bank speed to 0.9.
  • Pond: the mirror image is one piece that moves with the museum. The ripples and the leaves are a second layer. That layer scrolls row by row over the image.
  • Side canal (three scenes): the page draws it for each frame, column by column, from two long strips of house fronts. A thing at depth z moves at 1/z of the speed of the far bank, and all lines meet at the centre of the screen.
  • Loops: water 3 frames, clouds drift, the sun glitter in the sunset scene.
  • Drag a screen to move the camera.

Limits

  • Vincent has one size in the game. At 2× and 4× his pixels are larger than the pixels of the scenery.
  • The parts come from generators. A hand-made stage of the reference has more one-off objects.
  • Cars, boats, birds and leaves do not move yet. The street posts stand on the kerbstones only.
  • The museum is an own impression. It is not a survey of the real building.
  • Nothing in the game changed. This page is a study.
