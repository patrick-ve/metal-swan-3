## 1. Product summary

An original 2D arcade run-and-gun shooter for the browser, in the style of late-1990s arcade
games: side-scrolling missions, one-hit deaths, weapon crates, grenades, an automatic knife at
close range, hostages that hand out items, vehicles the player can ride, route choices, large
multi-part bosses, and 2-player co-op.

- Level 2 (`sewers`, user decision 2026-11-11, `docs/design/sewers.md`) is a 2D mission in De Wallen
  by night and the sewer under it, loaded lazily with its MISSION 2 card; the route is ams 1,
  sewers 2, rally 3, the fps floors 4, oldenzaal 5 (`flow-data.ts` `FPS_ROUTE`). It replaced `oat`
  (level 2 from 2026-10-31, deleted on 2026-11-12).
- Level 3 (`rally`, user decision 2026-10-22, level 2 until 2026-10-31, `docs/design/rally.md`) is a
  **3D race** drawn with WebGL2 (the first GPU code, in `*.browser.ts` only) with the look of the
  32-bit console era: low-poly meshes and palettised textures, all generated by code; the 2D HUD
  on top.
- All art is pixel art **generated by code** at boot (no image files at runtime). Exception (user
  decision 2026-10-16): the first-person boss suit's face comes from user art in `art-src/user/`;
  `tools/art/import-face.ts` turns it into a committed palette-index module
  (`src/game/content/fps/art/mecha-face.gen.ts`), so the game still loads no image file.
  Exception (user decision 2026-11-11): the scenery of mission `ams` in the canal look is a Python
  paint kit's layer stack (`tools/levelkit/`), BAKED to lossless PNG files at 1×, 2× and 4×
  (`public/art/ams/`, by `tools/levelkit/lk.py ams bake`, with a generated manifest
  `src/game/content/missions/ams/canal/manifest.gen.ts`). A new level built by
  `docs/LEVEL_GUIDE.md` gets its scenery the same way (`public/art/<mission>/`). A section opts in with
  `SectionDef.canal`; the game loads the files per section and art tier ahead of the camera
  (`src/engine/png.ts` decodes them, exact; `src/game/mission/canal/` stores and draws them) and
  draws the stack in place of that section's generated scenery. `?canal=0` (or a missing file)
  draws the generated scenery. Figures, enemies, pickups and effects stay generated. See
  `docs/api/canal.md`. A dark section may be baked twice as a LIT PAIR (the lit stack's manifest
  names its ambient one, `litOf`): the heroes' flashlights show the lit stack through a mask, and
  the light pass darkens the entities only (`docs/api/canal.md` §12; mission `sewers` c, d, e,
  `docs/api/sewers.md` §5).
- All sound effects and music are **synthesized** with Web Audio (no audio files). Exception
  (user decision 2026-10-14): the start screens (with the REPORT FOR DUTY drill callouts), mission
  `ams` and the first-person mode (the `mode:fps` group, user approval 2026-10-16) also ship
  sampled sound effects, music and dialogue (MP3 under `public/audio/`, generated with
  ElevenLabs); the synth stays the fallback for every id (§5.7). Mission `oldenzaal` (user
  decision 2026-10-21) has its own group `mission:oldenzaal` for the same pipeline; its takes are
  generated after the user's OK on the credit estimate (synth only until then).
- Content and design: `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md`. IP rules: `docs/ORIGINALITY.md` (mandatory reading).

## 2. Hard rules (all agents, all code)

1. **TypeScript strict** with `erasableSyntaxOnly` (no `enum`, no `namespace`, no constructor
   parameter properties — declare fields explicitly) and `verbatimModuleSyntax` (use
   `import type` for type-only imports). Avoid `any`; if unavoidable, comment why.
2. **No runtime dependencies.** Dev dependencies only (vite, vitest, tsx, playwright, typescript,
   @types/node). No external asset files (no PNG/MP3/WAV/font files). Everything is code.
   Exception (user decision 2026-10-14): MP3 samples in `public/audio/` for the start screens
   and mission `ams` (sample groups `boot`, `mission:ams`) and the first-person mode (sample
   group `mode:fps` under `public/audio/fps/`, generated after the user's OK on the credit
   estimate and shipped 2026-10-16; loaded by `loadFpsContent` behind the bridge / fps intro
   progress bar, never at boot) and mission `oldenzaal` (sample group `mission:oldenzaal` under
   `public/audio/oldenzaal/`, user decision 2026-10-21; generated after the user's OK on the
   credit estimate), listed in `src/game/content/samples/manifest.ts` (written by
   `tools/audio/`). Every sampled id keeps its synth version as the fallback, and headless runs
   never load them. Files kept by hand
   (the tool never touches them) join the groups in `src/game/content/samples/index.ts`:
   MECHA HASLEMA's clips (`public/audio/mayor/`) and the user's own music tracks
   (`public/audio/user/music/`, user decision 2026-10-16).
3. **Deterministic, headless-safe simulation.** Code in `src/engine/` and `src/game/` must not
   touch `window`, `document`, `navigator`, `localStorage`, `performance`, `AudioContext`,
   `requestAnimationFrame`, `Math.random`, `Date`, or `import.meta.env` — **except** in files
   named `*.browser.ts`, which may only be imported by `src/main.ts` or other `*.browser.ts`
   files. Gameplay randomness uses the seeded `world.rng`. Purely cosmetic randomness uses
   `world.fxRng` (also seeded) so headless runs stay reproducible. GPU code (WebGL2, mission
   `rally`) follows the same rule: `gl3d*.browser.ts`, created by `main.ts` and injected through
   `AppOptions.view3d`; the race's sim folders (`src/game/rally/`, `src/engine/r3/`,
   `src/game/content/rally/`) also avoid `Math.sin / cos / tan / atan2 / exp / log / pow / hypot`
   (the r3 trig tables; a vitest scans them).
4. **Fixed timestep 60 Hz.** All speeds are px/tick, all timers are ticks.
5. **Original content only.** Read `docs/ORIGINALITY.md` before writing any names, art, text or
   music.
6. **File ownership.** Each agent edits only the files and folders its task assigns. Other agents
   work in parallel in other folders. If you need a change in a file you do not own, make a local
   workaround inside your own files and list the needed change in your final summary.
7. **Fail soft.** Unknown sprite / sound / song / entity IDs must never crash the game: draw a
   magenta placeholder, play nothing, or spawn a placeholder entity, and `console.warn` once per ID.
   Content modules load with `Promise.allSettled`, so one broken module never stops the game.
8. **Quality gates before you finish:** `npm run typecheck` has zero errors in your files,
   `npm test` passes, and you looked at the visual output of your work (section 9).
9. **No CSS or asset imports** outside `src/main.ts`; no top-level `await` in `src/`.

## 3. Platform facts

- Logical resolution **384 × 216** (16:9). The display scales by the largest integer factor that
  fits (pixel-perfect), else best fit with nearest-neighbour. Letterbox is black.
- Coordinates in pixels, origin top-left, **y grows downward**.
- **High-resolution backbuffer.** All game code and all logic work in the logical 384 × 216
  pixels. The browser renderer draws into a backbuffer `res` = k times larger, where k follows the
  display's integer scale s (device px per logical px) and the GRAPHICS filter's factor f
  (`chooseRes()` in `render-common.ts`, `display.resFor(f)`): k = s up to `KMAX` = 6; above
  that the largest k ≤ 6 that f divides (4K, s = 10: k = 6 for `pixel` / `smooth`, 4 for
  `xbr`; 16-inch MacBook Pro fullscreen, s = 9: 6 / 4); k = 1 when the window is smaller than
  1×. The present step scales k → s by nearest neighbour at any ratio: every logical pixel edge
  is a whole device pixel for any k ≤ s, and with k = s or f | k every filtered pixel lands
  exactly where a direct nearest-neighbour scale to the display would put it (tested for s
  1–16 in `tests/engine/hires.test.ts`). Where s / f is not an integer (s = 3 or 5 with
  `smooth`: 1.5 / 2.5 device px per filtered pixel) the filtered pixels are uneven by nature of
  the display; the visual review found this invisible at 1080p (k 4, 5 and 6 look the same at
  1:1). `?kmax=` overrides KMAX (perf / debug). Resizes and DPR changes re-create the
  backbuffer.
  - Rects, lines, text, backgrounds (`parallax`), terrain chunks and `image()` buffers are
    enlarged as crisp k × k blocks (never filtered).
  - Registered sprites (`sprites.ts` ids) go through a pixel-art upscaler once per frame buffer
    (`upscale.ts`, factor f = 2–4) and are drawn over exactly their 1× footprint times k (the
    anchor and flips stay exact in logical pixels; at k not a multiple of f the upscaled pixels
    are enlarged by nearest neighbour to k / f). Never filtered (`filtersId`): ids under
    `UNFILTERED_PREFIXES` (`ui/`: icons, the HUD, lettering) except the character art in
    `FILTERED_UI_PREFIXES` (`ui/leader-`, `ui/ending-`, `ui/title-swan` …, so a scene does not
    mix two looks), the exact ids in `UNFILTERED_IDS` (`fx/explosion-l`, `fx/explosion-xl`,
    `decor/ams/fabel-sign`: dithered fire and script that the filters only speckle) and the
    font. Dense (2× and 4×) art (below) is never filtered either.
  - **Dense art (frame density, the mission ams flow).** A `Frame` has an optional `density` d
    (art px per logical px, default 1; dense art has d 2 or 4). A dense frame's buffer is d times
    as wide and high; its logical size (`frameW(f)` / `frameH(f)`), anchor `ox` / `oy` and points
    `pts` stay in LOGICAL px, so game logic reads the same numbers at every density.
    `defineHdSprite(id, (d) => frames)` (or `defineSprite(id, gen, { hd })`) adds ONE
    density-generic generator for d 2 and 4 to an existing id (per density: `defineHiSprite(id,
    gen, d)` / `{ hi, hi4 }`); `defineDenseAlias(id, target)` makes an id show a target's identical
    dense frames (built, held, counted, sent and packed once; the 1× frames stay its own; the alias
    must have the target's 1× geometry). One cache per density; `getFrames(id, d)` returns the
    highest density ≤ d the id has (4 → 2 → 1, never an upscale). The registry re-frames every
    dense frame to its 1× frame's logical rectangle, copies the 1× points and falls back to the
    next lower frames (a warning and a `spriteErrors()` entry `id@<d>x`) on a wrong frame count, a
    buffer size not divisible by d, a different point or a thrown error. The art density is 1
    except inside `MissionRunner.draw` and the gameplay HUD of a mission whose
    `MissionDef.artDensity` is above 1 (only `ams`: 4; the attract demo passes `artDensity: 1`);
    they draw at min(artDensity, the art tier) (`MissionRunner.artDensity`; `maxArtDensity` is the
    cap). Generators always run at density 1 (they ask for dense parts with `getFrames(id, d)`),
    and lazily built terrain chunks and background layers too. Drawing: with d | k each art px is
    an exact (k / d)² block; with k > d otherwise nearest neighbour (`blitScaled`; Canvas may pick
    the other pixel on ties); at k < d the renderers draw the reduction to the highest density ≤ k
    that divides d (`drawDensity`, `reducedFor`: a 4× frame at k 2 or 3 shows its 2× image, at k 1
    its 1× image, the top-left px of each block; flips mirror that image), so res 1 stays
    deterministic. `DrawOpts.src` is in logical px for any density. Boot generates only 1× frames;
    the mission intro card (and gameplay, for a direct start) starts `preloadMissionArt` (§5.4: the
    art pool builds the 2× set and, at tier 4, the working set; only in the browser app,
    `GameApp.preloadArt`; headless runs never preload); anything not ready is generated on first
    draw, except 4× frames in the browser (below). Checks: `tests/engine/hires-density.test.ts`,
    `tests/engine/density4.test.ts`, the geometry fixture (`tests/engine/hires.test.ts`) and the
    Canvas / software parity at k 1–8 (`tests/media/browser.test.ts`). Rules for the art:
    `docs/ART_GUIDE.md` "2x art (mission ams): the dense art contract" and "UHD art".
  - **Dense scenery (ams only).** Background layers and terrain have the same density path:
    `defineHdBgGenerator(id, (w, h, params, seed, d) => …)` (the 1× generator's logical
    arguments plus d; returns (d·w) × (d·h)) and the HD terrain painter `paintTerrainHd`
    (`setHdTerrainPainter`) with `defineHdTerrainMaterial(kind, id, paint(p, d, out, i, stride))`
    (terrain-art.ts: the same cell classification as 1×, so geometry and collision cannot change; a
    rect with no HD material gives null). `SectionView(section, terrain, seed, density)` (the
    runner passes its art density) draws each layer and chunk at the highest density ≤ min(its
    density, T) that has art and that k can show (4 needs 4 | k): a layer with
    `ParallaxOpts.density` d (it tiles by the logical size; offsets, factors and drift stay
    logical, sub-pixel scrolling is unchanged), a chunk as a density-d frame (cropped to its opaque
    part); at res 1 the 1× buffer is the exact image. The 1× buffers are always built first;
    density 1, m0–m5 and every 1× pixel stay byte-identical (`tests/art/scenery-hi.test.ts`,
    fixture `scenery-1x.json`). Fallbacks (wrong size, throw) draw the lower art and are listed by
    `hiSceneryErrors()`. Budget: `preloadMissionArt` also turns on `setDeferHiArt(true)`; the view
    then queues each dense layer / chunk as an art job (`queueArtJob`) and draws the lower art until
    the job ran (`runArtJobs(HI_ART_FRAME_BUDGET_MS)`, 8 ms per frame; the pool's main-thread
    fallback runs them first in its slices); it releases dense chunks `EVICT_BEHIND_CHUNKS` (2) or
    more chunks left of the view (ams scrolls forward), and a replaced view drops its jobs
    (`dispose`). Without deferral (headless tools, tests) dense scenery is built when first drawn.
    Measured in node at 4×: a layer median 1.1–13.2 ms (a cold first build up to 21 ms), 49–53 MB
    of layers per section (2×: 12–13 MB); a terrain chunk median 5.8–8.4 ms (at most 13.6),
    14–25 MB per section. Rules for the art: `docs/ART_GUIDE.md` "UHD backgrounds and terrain
    (mission ams)".
  - **Art tiers and 4× art (UHD art, mission ams flow).** The art tier T ∈ {1, 2, 4}
    (`artTier()` / `setArtTier(t)` in `sprites.ts`, default 2 for tools and tests) is the
    highest art density the display draws. main.ts picks T and the backbuffer res together
    (`display.tierRes(f, want)` → `chooseTierRes` in `render-common.ts`): AUTO gives tier 4 when
    the integer display scale s ≥ 8 (4K s = 10, 5K, Retina fullscreen s = 9) with k = 8
    (`KMAX_TIER4`; 4K keeps exact 2 × 2 blocks per 4× pixel and every filter factor divides
    k), tier 2 for 2 ≤ s < 8 with k ≤ `KMAX` = 6 as before, tier 1 below. Tier 4 keeps 4 | k (a
    forced 4× at s 5–7 gives k = 4) and AUTO keeps tier 2 under a `?kmax=` cap below 8. Option
    ART DETAIL (AUTO / 4X / 2X / 1X, `settings.artDetail`; the Options hint shows the tier in
    effect) and `?art=1|2|4` (wins until the option is changed). `MissionDef.artDensity` is the
    mission's HIGHEST density (ams: 4). At tier 4 the browser never generates 4× frames on the
    main thread (`setDeferDense(true)`): a missing one draws its 2× frame and the id is queued
    (`takeDenseQueue()`) for the art pool, which installs the result (`installFrames(id, 4, …)`);
    `hasFramesAt` / `framesDensityAt` peek without generating, `releaseDense(ids, 4)` /
    `denseBytes(4)` serve the working set (§5.4). Screens outside the mission (menus, select,
    ending) draw their own dense art at `screenTier(r)` through the screen art API
    (`scenes/base.ts`, docs/api/media.md §7), text uses the HD glyph tables at the same tier
    (`glyphDensity`, media.md §6) and the first-person view draws at density 2 at tiers 2 and 4
    (docs/design/fps.md §3.4). **Governor:** at tier 4 main.ts averages render + present over
    2 s windows (`FrameGovernor`); above 8 ms (software rendering of the k 8 backbuffer) it caps the tier
    at 2 (k ≤ 6) for the rest of the session and logs it with `console.info`. Checks:
    `tests/engine/density4.test.ts` (tier table s 1–16, fallback, validation, peek, deferral,
    release, preload, renderers, scenery, runner, governor, options) and the Canvas / software
    parity of 4× frames at k 1, 2, 4 and 8 (`tests/media/browser.test.ts`).
  - Modes (option GRAPHICS, URL `?gfx=`): `pixel` (no sprite filter, the default: the look the
    user prefers; saves without `SETTINGS_VERSION` 2 load GRAPHICS as `pixel` once, because
    earlier builds saved their old `smooth` default with the other options), `smooth` (MMPX 2×, palette-exact), `xbr` (xBR 4×, blending: smoother curves, new colours at edges;
    `decor/` sprites are drawn with `smooth` in this mode, because xBR rounds bricks and
    windows, blurs lettering and leaves seams between tiles). A filter whose factor is above k
    steps down: `xbr` → `smooth` → `pixel` (`modeAtRes`); the Options row then shows a hint.
    `spriteFilter()` in `render-common.ts` decides per sprite for both renderers. With the
    default sub-pixel policy the world and the background layers move in 1/k steps in every
    mode; only `off` gives the classic 1× image enlarged.
  - Sub-pixel policy (`SubpixelMode`, default `all`): the camera and every world draw snap to
    1/k (`Math.round(x · k)`), background layers with a parallax factor ≠ 1 scroll by the
    unrounded camera; rects keep whole-pixel sizes, lines whole logical pixels relative to the
    camera, screen-space draws whole logical pixels. The draw code of what mission ams shows
    passes UNROUNDED positions (logic never changes): the player (legs, torso, tints), the Swan
    (body, scuffs, overlay, neck) and her rider (`Player.drawRide`), the ams locals
    (`Enemy.fineDraw`), ams NPCs, ambient entities, breakables and decor (`SectionView`), the
    ams mayor (all body pieces, the lash x, the wig, the shadow) and her shots, projectiles,
    pickups, crates, hostages and their binds, fx sprites and shadows, the Swan's egg and sonic
    ring. Parts of one entity add whole-pixel offsets to one unrounded origin, so they never
    drift apart. Still rounded (whole logical px on the world grid, 1/k against the camera):
    the Legion enemies and other missions' entities (`Enemy.fineDraw` is off by default because
    subclasses draw extra parts after `super.draw`), `Player.drawSeated` (fleet vehicles),
    `Boss` / `Vehicle` base draws, rope segment rows, flame particles, orbiting dizzy feathers,
    weapon lines and fx rects. Measured at k = 5 (`tests/engine/hires-subpixel.test.ts`, mean
    |second difference| of on-screen x while walking right in ams-a): the player moves with
    0.022 px jitter (4 direction reversals in 300 ticks) under `all`, against 0.091 px / 6 with a
    whole-pixel world camera (`off` / `parallax`) and 0.818 px / 283 with `camera` (a sub-pixel
    camera over a pre-rounded player); background layers judder 0.080 px (`all`, `parallax`),
    0.474 px (`off`); terrain scroll 0.189 px against 0.950 px with a whole-pixel camera. Part
    offsets of the player, the Swan with rider and the mayor never leave the logical-pixel grid
    (0 off-grid draws of 3782 / 840 / 900). `off` = everything on whole pixels; `parallax` = only
    background layers in 1/k steps; `camera` = a 1/k camera over whole-pixel world draws.
  - The headless `SoftRenderer` and the simulation default to k = 1 (tests and bots are
    unchanged); `--res` / `--gfx` on `shot` and `arena` render the browser look headless. At
    res 1 every policy and mode gives the classic image pixel for pixel.
  - **The perspective floor (`SectionDef.floor`, docs/design/floor.md, docs/api/floor.md; `ams`
    and `oldenzaal` from 2026-10-25).** The floor band (the ground's top surface seen by a pinhole
    camera, from the back edge to the screen bottom) draws at the scenery's density rule
    (`SectionView.atRes(drawDensity(), res)`: 4 needs 4 | k, else 2 at k ≥ 2, else 1) into ONE band
    image: the WebGL2 program (`gl-floor.browser.ts`, `AppOptions.floorGpu`) or the CPU raster
    (`src/engine/floor/raster.ts`; the reference headless, the browser's fallback capped at density
    2), put in the frame in draw order (`Renderer.surface` / a streamed `frame`). Its textures are
    world-space chunks of 256 u at density 1, 2 or 4 whose rows are the flat floor's screen rows
    (box-filtered in v when baked: no mips, no shimmer) and small tiles with v-mips; sampling is
    sharp bilinear; the camera is the renderer's (1/k): every row scrolls at its own speed s(y) and
    the walk line exactly with the world. The chunks are art jobs (slices of 64 u at 2×, of 8 u at
    4×; the tiles and the rows' dense prop frames in slices too: every floor job under ~5 ms),
    queued at the section start (`SectionView.prepare`: the visible 1× set on the spot, the dense
    set as jobs; a direct start and the menu flow alike), prefetched ahead and released behind
    (`FLOOR_EVICT_BEHIND` 2 chunks, a 4× one after 1); the tiles are built and released by the
    camera's window. Outside `DENSE_BUDGET_BYTES`: the floor's own budget is ONE number,
    `SectionFloor.cpuBytes()` (textures, the canvas pool of the quads and side faces, row frames)
    ≤ 16 MB at tier 4; the mission preload warms the GL context (`FloorGpu.warm`). Sections without
    a floor draw byte-identically.
- Simulation tick: 60 Hz fixed step. Rendering runs once per animation frame with the latest
  state (no interpolation). Hidden tab → simulation pauses.

## 5. Engine (`src/engine/`)

The signatures below are the contract sketch. Implementers may refine names and add features,
but must document the final API in this file. The files `color.ts`, `pixel.ts` (stub),
`render.ts` and `audio.ts` were pre-written by the orchestrator as shared interfaces so the
simulation side and the media side can be built in parallel: **extend them, do not break them.**

### 5.1 Math, RNG, events
- `math.ts`: `clamp`, `lerp`, `approach(v, target, step)`, `sign`, `wrap`, `dist`, angle helpers,
  `Rect {x,y,w,h}`, `overlaps(a,b)`, `contains(r,x,y)`, `rectAt(anchorX, anchorY, rel)`.
- `rng.ts`: `class Rng(seed)` — `next()` in [0,1), `int(min,max)` inclusive, `range(min,max)`,
  `chance(p)`, `pick(arr)`, `sign()`, `fork(salt)`. Deterministic per seed.
- `events.ts`: small typed event bus (`on`, `off`, `emit`) used by World and the game layer.

### 5.2 Input
- Buttons: `'left' | 'right' | 'up' | 'down' | 'fire' | 'jump' | 'bomb' | 'start'`.
- `VirtualPad` per player, updated once per tick from one or more sources: `held(b)`,
  `pressed(b)` (went down this tick), `released(b)`, `heldTicks(b)`. Edge detection lives in the
  pad, so all sources only report a raw "held" set.
- Sources: `KeyboardSource` + `GamepadSource` (`input.browser.ts`), `ScriptedSource` (headless,
  tests, bot, attract mode). `GamepadSource` is the pure `PadSource` (`pads.ts`) polling
  `navigator.getGamepads()`; register it with `input.useGamepads(src)` (`input.gamepads`).
- Default keys — 1 player: arrows or WASD move, **Z/J fire, X/K jump, C/L bomb**, Enter start,
  Esc pause. 2 players: P1 = WASD + F/G/H (fire/jump/bomb) + 1 (start); P2 = arrows + ,/./
  (or numpad 1/2/3) + Enter. Bindings are rebindable and saved. Gamepads by position (standard
  indices): west fire, south jump, east bomb, START start, select pause; D-pad and left stick move.
- Gamepads for 2 players (Bluetooth controllers, 2026-10-20; [`docs/api/input.md`](api/input.md),
  spec `docs/design/ams-extras.md` §10): pad identity from `Gamepad.id` (Chrome, Firefox and
  Safari formats), families (PlayStation, Xbox, Nintendo / 8BitDo, generic) with their own labels
  (PlayStation ✕ ○ □ △ drawn as `ui/pad-ps`), profiles for non-standard pads and the hat-switch
  D-pad. Player slots claim pads and keep them (sticky: a reconnect gets its slot back, never a
  swap); 1 player: every pad drives player 1, START on a free pad makes player 2 join (GDD §4.9);
  2 players: each slot its own pad and pad map (`bindings.pad`, `bindings.pad2`), a third pad
  drives nobody. A lost pad pauses the game (`LostPadScene`). Rumble via `vibrationActuator`
  (OPTIONS RUMBLE). `keySplit`: a pad join leaves the keyboard whole for player 1.

### 5.3 Colours & pixel art toolkit (pure TS, no DOM)
- `color.ts`: `type Color = number` packed **0xRRGGBBAA** (alpha 0 = transparent), `rgba()`,
  `hex()`, channel getters, `toCss()`.
- `pixel.ts`: `class PixelBuffer { w; h; data: Uint32Array }` storing packed 0xRRGGBBAA values,
  with: `get/set`, `fill`, `rect`, `strokeRect`, `hline/vline`, `line` (Bresenham), `thickLine`,
  `circle(cx,cy,r,c,fill)`, `ellipse`, `poly(points,c)` (scanline fill), `blit(src,dx,dy,
  {flipX,flipY,map,alpha})`, `outline(c, {corners})` (1px outline outside opaque pixels),
  `bevel(ramp, {light})` (auto-shading: lighter near light-facing edges, darker away),
  `dither`, `noise(rng, …)`, `replace(from,to)`, `trimBounds()`, `clone()`, `scaled(n)`.
- `ascii(rows, key)` → PixelBuffer from strings ('.' or ' ' = transparent) for small hand-made
  details (faces, icons, emblems).
- `palette.ts`: ONE curated project palette (40–64 colours) of named ramps dark→light
  (`PAL.ink`, `PAL.steel[0..n]`, `PAL.olive`, `PAL.sand`, `PAL.skinA/B/C`, `PAL.fire`, `PAL.sky`,
  `PAL.snow`, …). **All art uses palette ramps** — this keeps many agents' art cohesive.

### 5.4 Sprites & animation
- `Frame { buf: PixelBuffer; ox: number; oy: number; pts?: Record<string, [number, number]> }`
  — `(ox, oy)` is the anchor inside the buffer; drawing a frame at world `(x, y)` puts its
  anchor on `(x, y)`. `pts` are named attachment points **relative to the anchor** for the
  unflipped frame (flip negates x). Standard point names: `hip`, `muzzle`, `hand`, `head`, `seat`.
  Optional `density` (2 or 4 for dense art, §3): `ox`, `oy`, `pts` stay logical; read the logical size
  with `frameW(f)` / `frameH(f)`, never `buf.w` / `buf.h` outside art code.
- `defineHdSprite(id, (d) => frames)` / `defineSprite(id, gen, { hd })` (one generator for 2× and
  4×), `defineHiSprite(id, gen, d)` / `{ hi, hi4 }`, `defineDenseAlias(id, target)`,
  `hasHiSprite`, `listHiSprites`, `setArtDensity` / `withArtDensity` / `artDensity`, `artTier` /
  `setArtTier`, `setHiArt(false)` (tools, tests), `getFrames(id, density?)`: dense art (§3).
- `defineSprite(id, gen, opts?)` registers a lazily generated frame strip. IDs are slash paths
  (`char/<id>/legs-run`, `enemy/rifleman/run`, `fx/explosion-m`, `item/crate`, `ui/icon-bomb`).
- `getFrames(id)` (generates on first use, then caches), `frameCount(id)`, `hasSprite(id)`,
  `listSprites(prefix?)`, `preloadSprites(prefixes?, onProgress?, opts?)` (yields between batches or time-budget slices; `first` / `last` prefixes set the order).
- `Animator`: `play(id, {ticksPerFrame, loop, restart})`, `update()`, `frame`, `index`, `done`.
- Missing ID → magenta placeholder frame + one `console.warn`.
- Renderers derive flipped and white "hit flash" variants lazily and cache them.
- **Boot preload** (2026-10-18): the boot scene generates only `bootArt(params)`
  (scenes/boot.ts): the start screens (`START_ART`), the select roster's characters (a direct
  start: only `?char=`, default recruit) and the `MissionDef.art` prefixes of the mission played
  first. Everything else is generated on first use; a mission without `art` makes the boot
  preload every sprite. `tests/game/boot-art.test.ts` checks that the list covers what the start
  screens, the attract loop and every part of ams draw. First visit: about 3 s instead of 12 s.
- **Sprite cache** (2026-10-18): the generators are pure, so their frames can be kept. A pack
  (`sprite-pack.ts`: JSON metadata + all pixels, gzip, shared buffers stay shared; the decoder
  streams, each decoded buffer gets its own ArrayBuffer, and a restore decodes only the ids it
  installs, so a released id frees its pixels) holds the ready frames of many ids;
  `readyFrames(id, d)`, `generatedSprites(d, prefix?)` and `installFrames(id, d, frames)` read and
  fill the registry without running a generator. `ArtCache` (src/game/art-cache.ts) keeps the
  packs 'boot' (1×, saved after the boot preload), 'hi' (2×, saved after the background 2×
  preload) and 'fps' (saved after the first-person preload) in a `ByteStore` (IndexedDB database
  `ms3-cache`, `byte-store.browser.ts`). Every pack carries the build tag `__MS3_BUILD__`
  (vite.config.ts: git commit + build time); a pack of another build, or one that does not
  decode, is a miss and is deleted. The boot restores 'boot' and 'hi' before its preload, which
  then skips the installed ids (repeat visit: about 1.4 s). Saves run in the background in
  256 KB chunks with a yield after each. Only the browser app in a production build has a cache
  (`app.artCache`); headless apps, the dev server and `?cache=0` run without one. UHD art
  (2026-10-19): the packs are 'boot' (1×), 'hi' (2×), 'fps', 'hd4-<section>', 'hd4-char-<hero>'
  and 'hd4-fps' (4×; `packDensity(name)`: 'hi' and 'hd2-…' 2, 'hd4-…' 4). Boot restores 'boot'
  and 'hi' only; the 4× packs are restored at the mission start and on a section change (gameplay.ts `missionArtSection`),
  installing only the working set's ids (`restore(name, keep?)`). The art pool (worker chunk
  `assets/art-worker-*.js`, precached by the service worker) builds the 2× set and the 4× working
  set off the main thread.
- **Art pool** (UHD art, 2026-10-19; `src/engine/art-pool.ts`, worker entry
  `src/game/art-worker.ts`, docs/api/media.md §5): min(cores − 1, 8) module workers (at least 1)
  load the content registry and build the ids they are given at density d; they send raw packs
  back as transferred ArrayBuffers (no SharedArrayBuffer, so no cross-origin isolation is
  needed), and the main thread installs them (`installFrames`). The frames are byte-identical to
  main-thread generation (tested). Ids of one family (`familyOf`: a hero's strip family — `legs`,
  `torso-pistol`, `torso-heavy`, `ride`, `helium`, `helium-wob`, the full-body rest —
  `enemy/<set>`, `boss/<id>`, `vehicle/<id>` …) go to the same worker, so shared parts stay warm
  there and one hero spreads over 7 workers. A dense alias is built and sent once, as its target.
  Without Worker, or after a worker fails, the ids are built by the budgeted main-thread preload
  (`MISSION_ART_BUDGET_MS` = 8 ms per frame). A build may be low priority (the bulk 2× set queues
  behind the tier-4 working set). Idle workers close after `POOL_IDLE_CLOSE_MS` = 5 s (one stays;
  a worker holds the content registry and its memoised art parts) and start again on demand. The
  screens and the first-person mode have small pools of their own (`SCREEN_POOL_MAX`,
  `FPS_POOL_MAX`: 4 workers at most), closed when idle.
- **Tier-4 working set** (`scenes/gameplay.ts`): at tier 4 only a working set is built at 4×
  (`denseWorkingSet`): the chosen heroes (all their strips but the menu-only select portraits;
  the other heroes never), the mission's
  `preloadFirst`, the current and the next section's `SectionDef.art` (ams: the decor and the
  fatbike pair sets are section art, never `preloadFirst`) and `boss/` only in the boss section.
  Ahead (the next section, every route of a split) it builds only what that section's first
  screens show: not its `fx/`, `boss/`, deferred strips nor its `SectionDef.artLate` (prefixes it
  shows only well into it, e.g. ams c2's bridges, Carré and stag party; they join on entry). The
  continue's reaction strips (`gloat`, `cower`, the stag riders' `sit-gloat` / `sit-cower`) are
  never in the set (`ON_DEMAND_4X`: built when drawn).
  The P2-only art (the Mute Swan) joins only with two players; with two players the fatbike
  youths (solo and pair sets, about 97 MB at 4×) keep their 2× art (`twoPlayer2x`). A 4× id drawn
  outside the set shows its 2× frame and is queued for the pool (`pumpDenseQueue`). On a section
  change (`missionArtSection`) the set moves on: the ids outside the new set are released first,
  as far as the budget needs room for the ids still to build, then the rest is restored from the
  packs or built, and the resident 4× frames are trimmed to `DENSE_BUDGET_BYTES` = 256 MB (least
  recently used first, never the heroes; also when everything was restored). One run per play: a
  new play (another mission, or a section before the run's) stops the old run first, and
  `stopDense()` (the play ends, or the tier falls below 4: the governor, ART DETAIL) drops the
  queue and releases every 4× frame but the fps art's. When the game proper starts, the menus' 4×
  art is released (`releaseScreenArt(keepForHeroes(…))`); the glitch bridge releases the
  mission's 4× set before the first-person mode (`releaseMissionDense()`, the fps art kept).
  Measured (node, 8 workers, 2026-10-20, after the polish group): the working set of section a
  builds in 3.3–3.8 s on a loaded machine (limit 6 s). After the arcade extras (2026-10-20, real
  4× bytes): the working set of one player is 212.3–251.6 MB in every ams section for each squad
  hero (worst: bob in section a, 251.6 MB), two players at most 243.0 MB;
  `tests/content/ams-dense-budget.test.ts` keeps every squad hero (1P) and hero pair (2P) under
  the budget with a 2 MB margin.
- **Offline cache** (2026-10-18): the production build writes a service worker `sw.js`
  (tools/sw/plugin.ts fills the template tools/sw/sw.js with every published file and a hash of
  its content). It installs the page, the scripts and the boot sample group, caches other files
  (mission and fps audio) when the game fetches them, serves a listed file from the cache only
  while its hash matches (a deploy downloads only changed files), loads the page from the
  network first and from the cache when offline. main.ts registers it once the boot scene is
  done (its downloads then come from the HTTP cache); `?cache=0` removes it.

### 5.5 Rendering
`render.ts` defines `Renderer`. Implementations: `render-canvas.browser.ts` (Canvas 2D) and
`render-soft.ts` (pure TS into a PixelBuffer; used by node tools and tests). Both must give the
same image for the same calls. Key members (see the file for the full interface):
`begin(clear)`, `end()`, `setCamera(x,y)`, `sprite(id, frameIndex, x, y, opts)`,
`frame(f, x, y, opts)`, `image(buf, x, y, opts)`, `rect(x,y,w,h,color,opts)`,
`text(str, x, y, opts)`, `parallax(buf, factorX, factorY, offsetY, opts)`, `fade(color, alpha)`.
`DrawOpts`: `flipX`, `flash` (white silhouette), `alpha`, `screen` (ignore camera), `blend:'add'`,
`stream` (the buffer changes most frames, e.g. the first-person view: the canvas renderer keeps
one streaming slot per buffer outside the texture cache instead of caching it; `docs/api/media.md`).
Screen shake is a camera offset, not a renderer feature. Every renderer also has `res` (backbuffer
px per logical px, §3), `mode` / `setMode()` (sprite filter) and `subpixel` / `setSubpixel()`;
`w`, `h`, `camX`, `camY` and all draw coordinates stay logical.
Optional, additive: THE LAYER (`layerBegin(box)`, `layerMask`, `layerShade`, `layerEnd`; both
renderers have it), an offscreen layer for the lit pair's masked composite and the entity-only
darkness of mission `sewers`; a caller must work without it (`docs/api/canal.md` §12,
`docs/api/sewers.md` §5).

### 5.6 Fonts
`font.ts`: original bitmap fonts in code — `small` (≈5×7, caps, digits, punctuation) and `big`
(8×8 bold, titles). `measure(text, font)`. Colour, shadow/outline and alignment supported.

### 5.7 Audio
`audio.ts` defines `AudioOut` (`sfx`, `music`, `voice`, `setVolume`, `stopAll`; optional
`pauseMusic` / `resumeMusic`, `sfxLoop(key, id)` / `stopSfx(key)` for engines and drones,
`duckMusic(factor)`, `setMuted`, `suspend` / `resume`) and `NullAudio` (logs calls for tests).
`audio.browser.ts` implements Web Audio (context created/resumed on the first user gesture).
Music fails soft: an unknown or broken song logs one warning and the song that is playing goes
on; asking for the current song again keeps it going (a paused one resumes). The game never stops
on an audio error.
- `defineSfx(id, recipe)`: declarative synth recipes (oscillator layers, noise, pitch/filter
  envelopes, ADSR, distortion/bit-crush), pre-rendered once with `OfflineAudioContext`. Voice
  limits per sound and global.
- `defineSong(id, song)`: small tracker format — bpm, rows per beat, per-channel pattern strings
  (`'C4 . . E4 | G4 - - .'`), instruments (pulse 12.5/25/50 %, triangle, saw, noise kick/snare/
  hat, simple FM bell), order list and loop point; scheduled with a look-ahead timer.
  **Songs must be original compositions.**
- `voice(line)`: announcer via `speechSynthesis` if available and enabled, else a short jingle.
- Samples (exception of 2026-10-14: the start screens with the boot drill callouts, `ams`, and —
  2026-10-16 — the first-person mode; docs/api/media.md §9.5, docs/api/audio-pipeline.md):
  `samples.ts` (pure) holds the manifest types, `defineSampleGroup` and `voiceKey(text)`;
  `samples.browser.ts` fetches and decodes a group (`AudioOut.loadSamples?(group)`: 'boot' in the
  boot scene, `MissionDef.audioGroup` 'mission:ams' on the intro card, 'mode:fps' by
  `loadFpsContent` with the fps mode only — never at boot; 'mission:oldenzaal' (2026-10-21) on
  oldenzaal's intro card like 'mission:ams'). The groups are `SAMPLE_GROUPS` in `samples.ts`, the
  one list the audio tools read (`tools/audio/lib.ts` `GROUP_DIRS`: one folder per group under
  `public/audio/`; `LAZY_GROUPS` 'mode:fps' and 'mission:oldenzaal' appear in the manifest only
  once something of them is picked). A loaded sample skins an
  existing sfx id, song id or spoken line; while it is missing, loading or failed the synth (or
  the speech announcer) plays. `say?(text)` voices speech bubble lines that have a sample (no
  fallback). Voice samples duck the music. NullAudio and the sim stats have no `loadSamples` /
  `say`, so logs, sims and game logic do not change.

### 5.8 Camera
`camera.ts`: `x, y` (top-left), `follow(targets)`, forward **ratchet** (never scrolls back
against the section's scroll direction), clamp to section bounds, `lock(minX, maxX)` /
`unlock()` for arena fights, vertical follow with dead-zone for tall sections,
`shake(amount, ticks)`. Players stay inside the view (invisible walls at the view edges).
Drawing uses `renderer.setCamera(camera.viewX, camera.viewY)` (unrounded, shake included); the
renderer snaps it to its grid (§3). `renderX` / `renderY` are the whole-pixel values.

### 5.9 Terrain & physics
`terrain.ts` — static geometry of one section:
- `ground`: polylines (points sorted by x). Everything below a polyline is solid. Slopes up to
  about 1:1 are walkable; steeper parts act as walls.
- `platforms`: one-way polylines (land from above, jump through from below, drop through with
  **down + jump**).
- `solids`: axis-aligned rects that block from all sides.
- `hazards`: rects `{kind: 'pit' | 'water' | 'spikes' | 'lava' | 'kill'}`.
- Dynamic solids: entities can act as moving platforms / blocks (elevators, tank hulls,
  destructible walls) via the world.
- Queries: `surfaceY(x, yFrom, yTo, opts)`, `isSolid(x, y)`, `lineOfSight(x0,y0,x1,y1)`,
  `hazardAt(rect)`, and `cellAt(x, y): 0 | 1 | 2 | 3` (empty / ground / one-way platform band
  about 4 px thick under each platform line / solid rect) — the terrain painter samples this.

`physics.ts` — `Body` (anchor at feet centre: `x, y, vx, vy, w, h, onGround, dropThrough`) and
`moveBody(body, terrain, world?)`: slope following (step up / snap down), landing only when
falling onto a surface from above, ceilings, walls, one-way platforms, drop-through, and
sub-stepping so fast bodies never tunnel. Returns contact flags (`landed`, `hitWall`,
`hitCeiling`, `onGround`). Constants come from `src/game/tuning.ts`. **Unit tests required.**

### 5.10 Entities, World, collisions
```ts
type Team = 'player' | 'enemy' | 'neutral';
type DamageKind = 'bullet' | 'explosion' | 'fire' | 'knife' | 'laser' | 'crush' | 'impact';
interface Damage { amount: number; kind: DamageKind; dirX: number; source?: Entity; owner?: number }
```
`Entity` (abstract): `id`, `type`, `world`, `x, y, vx, vy`, `hitbox` (relative to anchor),
`team`, `tags`, `hp/maxHp`, `facing: 1 | -1`, `z`, `age`, `dead`, `invulnTicks`, `flashTicks`,
`update()`, `draw(r)`, `hurtboxes()` (world-space; bosses return several), `onHit(d)` (returns
true if the hit was absorbed), `die(d?)`, `onRemove()`, `despawnMargin`.

`World`: `entities`, `players`, `terrain`, `camera`, `particles`, `scripts`, `rng`, `fxRng`,
`tick`, `events`, `audio`. `spawn(id, x, y, params?)` via the entity registry (unknown id →
placeholder entity). Queries: `byTag`, `query(rect, filter)`, `nearestPlayer(x, y)`,
`alivePlayers()`, `onScreen(entity, margin)`. `damageArea(shape, damage, team)` for explosions.
`drawHook: ((r, e) => boolean) | null` (draw only): when it returns true the world skips that
entity's own draw; the canal view sets it so an entity with a lit pair's canal sprite draws like
the scenery (`docs/api/canal.md` §12).
Tick order: scripts → entities update (insertion order) → projectile vs hurtbox pass (by team) →
contact damage → particles → camera → cleanup (remove dead, despawn far off-screen).

### 5.11 Scripts (coroutines)
`script.ts`: generator coroutines for level events and boss patterns.
```ts
type Wait = number | (() => boolean) | void;           // ticks | predicate | next tick
type ScriptFn<C> = (ctx: C) => Generator<Wait, void, unknown>;
world.scripts.run(fn, { owner?: Entity })              // auto-cancel when the owner dies
```
Helpers: `wait(t)`, `until(pred)`, `waitCamera(x)`, `waitAllDead(list | tag)`, `all(...)`,
`race(...)`.

### 5.12 Particles & FX
`particles.ts`: pooled particles (pixel / rect / sprite-frame based): debris with gravity and
bounce, sparks, smoke, shell casings, dust, embers. Explosions are an animated fx entity +
particles + shake + sound, via a game-level helper; explosion damage goes through
`world.damageArea`.

### 5.13 Scenes, storage, loop, debug
- `scene.ts`: stack-based `SceneManager` (`push`, `pop`, `replace`, fade transitions); a scene
  has `enter`, `exit`, `update`, `draw`.
- `storage.ts`: `Store` interface + memory implementation; `storage.browser.ts` uses
  localStorage inside try/catch. Settings, bindings, high scores.
- `loop.ts`: pure fixed-step accumulator; `loop.browser.ts`: requestAnimationFrame driver.
- `debug.ts`: overlay toggles — F1 hitboxes, F2 fps/entity counts, F3 god mode, F4 skip to the
  next section/boss, F5 give all weapons. URL params (parsed in main.ts):
  `?mission=m1&section=b&god=1&debug=1&seed=42&players=2&char=<id>&weapon=<id>&boss=1&mute=1`,
  plus `difficulty=easy|normal|hard`, `gfx=pixel|smooth|xbr` (GRAPHICS for this session, §3; the
  older spelling `res=` is still read) and
  `kmax=1..12` (highest backbuffer resolution; perf / debug).

## 6. Game layer (`src/game/`)

- `tuning.ts`: physics and gameplay constants (gravity, run speed, jump, coyote/buffer ticks,
  invulnerability…), informed by `docs/research/mechanics.md`.
- `session.ts`: `GameSession` — players (character, lives, score, bombs, weapon + ammo, rescued
  hostages), credits/continues, difficulty, mission/section/checkpoint, stats.
- `registry.ts`: `defineEntity(id, factory, meta)`, `defineWeapon(def)`, `defineCharacter(def)`,
  `defineMission(def)`; re-exports `defineSprite`, `defineSfx`, `defineSong`. Missing → placeholder.
- `player/`: Player entity — state machine; split upper/lower body; aim forward/up/down (down
  only in the air); crouch, crouch-walk, jump, fall, drop-through; shoot; throw bomb; automatic
  knife when an enemy is in melee range; mount/dismount vehicles; death and respawn with
  invulnerability blink; current weapon is lost on death (back to pistol).
- Base classes: `Projectile`, `Enemy`, `Boss` (multi-part hurtboxes, phases, damage states,
  death sequence), `Vehicle` (mountable, HP cells, own weapons, eject + ram attack), `Pickup`,
  `Hostage`.
- `hud.ts` (screen HUD + world-space speech bubbles and score pop-ups, all requested through
  `HudState` in `context.ts`), `settings.ts` (options content can read), `scenes/` (Boot, Title,
  Gameplay + Pause, Continue/GameOver, MissionComplete; Select, MissionIntro, Ending, Options and
  HighScores belong to the UI agent), `mission/` (mission runtime: sections, branches,
  checkpoints, the timer, auto-scroll, respawn rules, boss start/end, tally stats), `water.ts`
  (floating on water hazards).
- **Shared hooks for content** (details in `docs/api/game.md`): the enemy **reaction protocol**
  (`enemy.react(kind, ticks)` / `reactInArea` — anthem salutes, HONK ears, the JINGLE queue, SAY
  CHEESE …), shared enemy rules (attack tokens, `telegraphTicks`, gag awareness), the boss **part
  model** (`required` / `knifeable` / `contact` parts, `sequence`, `intermission`), vehicle rules
  (½ / 1 cell hits, eject window, ram = fire + jump, special = fire + bomb), status effects with
  physics / input / art / sound hooks (`defineStatus`), erasable enemy shots
  (`eraseEnemyShots`), damage `weapon` ids and `tags`, HUD bubbles / callouts / flashes, and
  per-section respawn rules, start vehicles, allowed vehicles and ice.
- `art/`: `humanoid.ts` (parametric human figure: style {skin, hair, headgear, top, pants,
  boots, build, accessories} + pose {limb angles, lean, crouch, aim, weapon pose}), `machines.ts`
  (wheels, treads, plates, rivets, barrels, rotors), `bg.ts` (skies, mountains, clouds, tree
  lines, skylines, parallax layer builders), `terrain-art.ts` (fills for ground polygons with
  top edges: dirt, sand, snow, rock, metal, wood…).
- **The perspective floor** (`mission/floor/`, engine part `src/engine/floor/`; spec
  `docs/design/floor.md`, API `docs/api/floor.md`): an ART-ONLY opt-in, `SectionDef.floor:
  FloorDef` (the camera, the floor painter by registered id, height-field features over the walk
  profile — kerbs, steps, cuts, decks —, prop rows, canopies / slabs / roofs, top faces, openings,
  face groups — the side faces of row-end houses, boats, cars, a raised bridge leaf, in perspective
  and only toward the screen's centre (`floor/faces.ts`) —, entity kinds with faces and depth steps,
  decor depths, the shadow strength). `SectionView` builds a `SectionFloor` (the mesh — watertight:
  no T-junction, no sliver quad, so a GPU's vertex snap opens no crack —, the texture store, the CPU
  raster) and draws in the floor's order: non-sky layers lifted by the floor's depth
  behind the feet, `backHook`, the band, back decor and back rows at their depths, canopies and top
  faces, walk-plane decor, terrain chunks with EMPTY ground cells, contact shadows (the runner calls
  `view.drawShadows` before `world.draw`), front decor and front rows. Decor marked
  `DecorDef.floorOnly` (the floor's own railings, facades) is drawn only with a floor: every no-floor
  path (none, `--floor-off`, a failed build) draws the section as before the floor. Render-only hooks:

### 6.1 Human sprite contract (shared by art and logic agents)

Sizes: a standing adult is about **34–38 px** tall (feet to top of head/headgear), crouching
about 24–27 px. Player hurtbox 14×30 standing, 16×20 crouching (the physics body stays 14 px
wide). Enemy soldiers: 14×28 standing, 14×26 crouching (the crouched figure is ~27 px tall, so
standing shots hit it). All frames are anchored at the **feet centre** unless noted; lying death
frames rest on the ground line.

Playable characters — `char/<id>/…`:
- Lower body: `legs-idle` (4f), `legs-run` (12f, 3 ticks scaled by `strideRate`), `legs-jump`
  (4f), `legs-fall` (2f loop), `legs-land` (3f), `legs-crouch`, `legs-crouch-walk` (6f),
  `legs-crouch-in` (3f) and `legs-crouch-out` (2f, draw-only transitions), `legs-dangle` (6f,
  the parachute drop). **Every legs frame has a `hip` point** (where the torso attaches; it bobs
  during the run; idle and crouch keep it constant).
- Upper body, anchored at the **hip**, for weapon pose `<w>` ∈ `pistol | heavy`:
  `torso-<w>-idle` (≥2f), `torso-<w>-shoot` (≥2f), `torso-<w>-up`, `torso-<w>-shoot-up` (≥2f),
  `torso-<w>-down`, `torso-<w>-shoot-down` (≥2f), `torso-<w>-throw` (≥4f), `torso-<w>-knife`
  (≥4f); the 45° up diagonal for both poses: `torso-<w>-diag-up` and `torso-<w>-shoot-diag-up`
  (≥2f; up + left/right, GDD §4.3); heavy only: `torso-heavy-diag-down` and
  `torso-heavy-shoot-diag-down` (the machine-gun sweep). Aims between 22.5° and 67.5° use the
  diagonal strips when registered (else the nearest of forward / up / down). Aim and shoot frames have a `muzzle`
  point; throw frames have a `hand` point. Animation wave (mission ams): `torso-<w>-idle` 4f,
  `-throw` 6f (release frame and `hand` unchanged at index 2), `-knife` 6f and `-knife-b` 6f
  (anticipation first, sum 14 ticks), and outside the Helium set `torso-<w>-run` (12f, synced to
  the legs), `-crouch` (4f), `-crouch-shoot` (2f), `-crouch-throw` (6f), `-crouch-knife` (6f).
  Gameplay reads the steady-state strips (`muzzleWorld`, `handWorld`); transitions are
  draw-only timers. A status effect may swap any strip for
  `<art>/<infix>-<anim>` (e.g. `helium-legs-run`) when that variant is registered.
- Full body (feet anchor): `die` (≥6f; players 12f), `die-burn` (≥4f), optional `die-blast`
  (explosions; players 10f), `victory` (≥2f; players 8f), `spawn` (≥3f; players 6f), `bored`
  (≥4f, optional idle fidget), `mount` (≥2f); `portrait` (1f, about 32×32, for menus).
  Animation wave (players): `flip` (14f, the running jump), `mount-hop` (5f, last frame has
  `hip`), `die-drown` (12f, anchor = the water surface, cropped there), `die-flat` (7f, crush),
  `fidget-a` / `fidget-b` (9–14f), `timeout-yawn` (8f), `timeout-sleep` (4f loop). Deferred
  (`SpriteOpts.defer`, made on first use / last in the dense preload): the fidgets, the time-out
  strips, `die-drown`, `die-flat`, `mount-hop`. FX used by the player: `fx/chute` (6f loop),
  `fx/chute-collapse` (8f; anchor = rim centre, point `harness` [0, 16]), `fx/snore` (6f,
  deferred; drawn at the `head` point).
- **Rider strips for open-seat mounts (the Swan)**, upper body only, **hip anchor**, rig part
  `'upper'`, `weapon: 'none'`, points `head` and `hand` (no `muzzle`): `ride-sit` (2f, 20 ticks,
  loop), `ride-lean` (2f, 6, loop), `ride-up` (1f), `ride-hunch` (1f), `ride-hold` (2f, 4, loop),
  `ride-brace` (1f), `ride-duck` (1f), `ride-ears` (2f, 6, loop), `ride-cheer` (2f, 8, loop) —
  9 strips, 14 frames, for every playable character and `recruit`. Both hands hold the handle
  grips, except `ride-ears` (both hands open on the ears) and `ride-cheer` (near fist raised).
  No `mount-duck` frame (`ride-duck` replaces it). Pose tables and arm targets:
  `docs/design/swan-mount/look.md` §6.4. No `helium-ride-*` (a Helium Head player cannot mount).
- **Open-seat mounts:** the vehicle's `seat` point is the rider's **hip** (for cockpit vehicles it
  is the driver's feet). The rider is drawn with `Player.drawRide(r, x, y, flipX, pose, frame?)`
  (`RidePose` = the nine names above), with the invulnerability blink and the fallback chain
  `ride-<pose>` → `ride-sit` → `mount` frame 1 drawn 17 px lower (look.md §6.5–§6.6). The
  driver's anchor is not the seat: an open-seat mount overrides the protected
  `Vehicle.syncDriver()` and puts the mounted player's x, y on its own feet anchor (a flyer such
  as the Trumpeter: 15 px below its hull centre). Enemy AI reads a player's y as the feet
  (`Rifleman.upShot`, `Enemy.aimAt` and `canSee` at y − 15), so it then sees a grounded target.
  The `seat` point still places the rider and starts the eject.

Enemy soldiers — `enemy/<id>/…` (full body): `idle` (≥2f), `gag` (optional idle comedy, ≥4f),
`alert` (≥2f), `walk` (≥6f), `run` (≥8f), `crouch`, `shoot` (≥2f, `muzzle`), `crouch-shoot`
(≥2f, `muzzle`), `throw` (≥4f, `hand`), `panic` (≥4f), `die` (≥5f), `die-blast` (≥4f),
`die-burn` (≥4f), `die-knife` (≥4f). Optional (the game falls back when missing): `die-crush`,
`die-shatter`, and the reaction poses `salute`, `dance`, `confused`, `frozen`, `queue`, `ears`,
`photo-pose`, `stumble` (GDD §9.0).

Hostages — `item/hostage/…` (or `item/hostage/<kind>/…`): `tied` (≥2f), `freed` (≥3f), `walk`
(≥6f), `give` (≥4f, `hand`), `run` (≥6f), optional `bow`.

**Dense art** (UHD, the mission ams flow): a strip of this contract may also have 2× and 4×
frames (every `char/<id>/…` strip does: `hd: (d) => gen(d)`). They have exactly the 1× frame's
logical size, anchor and points (§3), so this contract and every number in it hold at every
density; logic never reads art pixels.

### 6.2 Mission data format (contract for mission agents)
```ts
interface MissionDef {
  id: string;                         // 'm0'..'m5'
  name: string; subtitle?: string;
  music: string; bossMusic?: string;
  start: string;                      // first section id
  sections: Record<string, SectionDef>;
}
interface SectionDef {
  id: string;
  width: number; height: number;      // px; height ≥ 216 (taller = vertical scrolling)
  scroll: 'right' | 'up' | 'down' | 'free';
  background: BackgroundDef;          // parallax layers from procedural generators
  terrain: TerrainDef;                // ground polylines, platforms, solids, hazards
  terrainStyle: TerrainStyleDef;      // how ground polygons and platforms are painted
  decor?: DecorDef[];                 // static props behind / in front
  playerStart: { x: number; y: number };
  checkpoints?: number[];             // camera-centre x ('right'), or y in 'up' / 'down' sections
  timeLimit?: number;                 // HUD counts of 256 ticks (≈ 4.3 s), default 60, 0 = none
  script: ScriptFn<SectionContext>;   // spawns, camera locks, set pieces, exits, boss
  exits?: ExitDef[];                  // { rect, to: sectionId, transition } — route choices
  respawn?: (p, ctx) => {x, y} | void;// section respawn rule (default: nearest safe floor in view)
  startVehicle?: string;              // players start (and respawn) mounted in this vehicle
  vehicles?: string[];                // vehicles that may be carried INTO this section
  iceRanges?: [number, number][];     // icy ground x ranges (players and vehicles slide)
}
```
The real types live in `src/game/mission/types.ts` (folder `src/game/mission/`); see
`docs/api/game.md` §10 for `SectionContext` (spawning, `enter` that never lands over a pit,
camera locks, `autoScroll(speed)`, `bossIntro(name, {subtitle})` which refills and pauses the
timer, callouts, exits) and `MissionDef` (`hostages` per section for the tally, `startBanner`,
`announce`, `clearLine`, `artDensity` — the highest dense art it draws, ams 4, §3; `preloadFirst` /
`preloadLast` — the order of its dense preload; `SectionDef.art` — what a section draws, the
tier-4 working set, §5.4; `SectionDef.artLate` — the part of `art` the section before it never
builds ahead).
Builders keep terrain readable, e.g. `ground().at(0, 170).flat(400).slope(200, -40).gap(48)…`.

`BackgroundDef` and `TerrainStyleDef` are defined in **`src/game/art/art-registry.ts`** (a
pre-written contract):
- A background is `{ sky, layers: BgLayerDef[] }`; each layer names a generator registered with
  `defineBgGenerator(id, gen)` plus parallax factors. Standard generator ids (art agent):
  `sky-gradient`, `clouds`, `mountains`, `hills`, `treeline`, `jungle`, `city`, `ruins`, `dunes`,
  `ice-peaks`, `sea`. Mission agents may register their own generators.
- Terrain is painted in chunks by the painter set with `setTerrainPainter`, which samples
  `terrain.cellAt(x, y)`. A style names materials. Standard material ids (art agent): ground —
  `dirt`, `sand`, `rock`, `snow`, `ice`, `mud`, `metal`, `concrete`; edge — `grass`, `snow`,
  `sand`, `moss`, `rust`, `tiles`; platform — `wood`, `metal`, `rock`, `ice`; solid — `metal`,
  `concrete`, `crate`, `rock`, `brick`.
- Missing generators / painter fall back to flat placeholder art.

`DecorDef` (static props) is defined by the mission runtime: at minimum
`{ sprite: string; x: number; y: number; frame?: number; flipX?: boolean; front?: boolean }`.

## 7. Content manifest & registration

`src/game/content/index.ts` (owned by the orchestrator) maps manifest IDs to lazy imports.
At boot, `loadContent()` imports them with `Promise.allSettled`, logs failures, and the game
continues with placeholders. Every category folder has two entry files:

- `index.ts` — gameplay definitions (entities, weapons, characters…) for that category.
- `art.ts` — sprite definitions for that category (so art and logic can be built separately and
  `npm run sprites` can load art alone).

Mission and boss folders have a single `index.ts` that imports its own art. A category split
into sub-areas (`infantry/rhythm`, `vehicles/swan` …) gives every sub-area its own manifest
entries, so one broken sub-area never takes its siblings down; the category's top-level
`index.ts` / `art.ts` hold only shared helpers (except `infantry/index.ts`, which is `rifleman`).

## 8. Conventions

- z-order: decor-back 10 < items 20 < enemies 30 < vehicles 35 < players 40 < projectiles 50 <
  fx 60 < decor-front 80 < HUD.
- Teams: player shots and player explosions hurt only the `enemy` team (no friendly fire); enemy
  shots hurt the `player` team (players and player vehicles). Hostages are neutral and can't die.
- Conventional tags: `player`, `enemy`, `shootable`, `shot`, `soft` (knifeable), `boss`,
  `vehicle`, `hostage`, `cover` (riflemen hide behind it), `erasable` (enemy shots that tuba
  rings, HONK, the respawn POOF and boss intermissions remove), `legion` (Legion soldiers: they
  accept the anthem / JINGLE / HONK reactions). A prop built on `Enemy` is tagged `enemy` and
  holds `ctx.waitClear()` — remove the tag (or use another base) for props that must not.
- Damage: `Damage.kind` says what hit (`bullet explosion fire knife laser crush impact`),
  `Damage.weapon` which weapon, `Damage.tags` extra facts (`heavy`, `boss`, `shockwave`, `sonic`,
  `spread`, `hazard`; test with `damageHas`). The weapon → kind + tags table is in
  `docs/api/game.md` §6.
- Fairness (GDD §4.11): telegraphs ≥ 18 ticks (`telegraphTicks`), no attack from outside the
  view (check at the shot, not only at the wind-up), attack tokens, gags are free time.
- Balance numbers live in `GAME_DESIGN.md`, entity defs, and `tuning.ts`.
- Comments: short; explain non-obvious maths and timings.

## 10. Performance budget

60 fps on a mid-range laptop with ≤ 300 active entities and ≤ 800 particles. No per-tick
allocations in hot paths (pool projectiles and particles). Sprites and backgrounds are converted
to canvases once and cached: one LRU texture cache in the canvas renderer with a pixel budget
(`TEXTURE_BUDGET_PX` = 48 M px of canvases and upscaled buffers, ≈ 190 MB; a full ams run needs
22 M at factor 2 and 78 M at factor 4, so xbr evicts and re-scales about 7 % of its sprites).
Upscaling is lazy and limited to `SCALE_BUDGET_MS` = 4 ms per frame; a sprite not upscaled yet
draws from its 1× texture for that frame. The render cost grows with the backbuffer area, not
with the filter (`npm run perf`, ams boss, render + present per frame, avg ms): on the CPU
rasteriser (headless SwiftShader) k = 1: 0.8, k = 5: 8.5, k = 6: 12.1, k = 10: 33.5 (27 fps); on
the GPU (`--gpu`, Apple M5) 0.3–0.37 at every k up to 10, 60 fps, xbr p95 ≤ 0.6. KMAX = 6
caps the CPU cost: 4K draws at k = 6 with `smooth` (12.3 ms CPU, 57 fps on SwiftShader; GPU 0.37)
and k = 4 with `xbr` (5.6 ms CPU); 1080p at k = 5 (8.6 ms CPU, GPU 0.33). `?kmax=4` is the knob
for a slow software-rendered machine.

Art tier 4 (§3): the texture budget follows k (`textureBudgetFor(k)`: 48 M px up to k = 7,
`TEXTURE_BUDGET_PX_K8` = 96 M px ≈ 384 MB at k ≥ 8, since 4× frames hold four times the pixels of
2× frames). Texture uploads are one 32-bit byte-order swizzle per pixel into a reused ImageData
(no per-channel writes), and dense frames have an upload budget (`UPLOAD_BUDGET_MS` = 6 ms per
frame): once spent, a dense frame whose texture is not uploaded yet draws its 1× reduction for
that frame and is uploaded on a later one (`TextureStats.uploadMs` / `uploadDeferred`). 4×
frames are never generated on the main thread during play (the art pool builds them, §5.4).
